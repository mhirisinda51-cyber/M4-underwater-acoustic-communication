\# Preliminary Results



\## 1. Transmission loss



The model estimates underwater acoustic transmission loss using spherical spreading

(with a spreading factor of 20) and Thorp absorption. Loss increases with distance

and frequency. Higher frequencies therefore have a shorter useful communication

range under the current model assumptions.



\## 2. Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)



The first SNR model uses an assumed source level of 190 dB and noise level of 60 dB.

These are illustrative inputs, not measured values or specifications for a particular

acoustic modem.



The estimated SNR decreases as distance and frequency increase. At 10 km, the

current model estimates approximately 46.18 dB at 5 kHz, 38.13 dB at 10 kHz,

8.66 dB at 20 kHz, and -32.80 dB at 30 kHz.



\## 3. Theoretical capacity



The Shannon capacity model uses a temporary bandwidth of 1,000 Hz. Its results are

theoretical upper bounds, not guaranteed practical modem data rates.



The estimated capacity decreases with distance and frequency. At 10 km, the model

estimates approximately 15.34 kbps at 5 kHz, 12.67 kbps at 10 kHz, 3.06 kbps at

20 kHz, and approximately 0.8 bps at 30 kHz.



\## 4. Propagation delay



Using a sound speed of 1,500 m/s, the estimated one-way propagation delay is

approximately 0.67 seconds per kilometre. This gives about 3.33 seconds at 5 km

and 6.67 seconds at 10 km, before accounting for processing or protocol delays.



\## 5. Limitations and next steps



These are preliminary results based on simplified assumptions. The source level,

noise model, bandwidth, spreading model, and practical modem constraints must be

checked before drawing final conclusions. The next step is to compare the model

with published modem specifications under comparable operating conditions.
